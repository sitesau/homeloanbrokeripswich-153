Questions to Take Into a Broker Review

A productive broker review transforms general uncertainty into specific, actionable checks. While borrowers don't need to memorise every lender rule, they should arrive with sufficient detail for the broker to effectively explore options. Key questions include:

  • What is the exact deposit amount available, and how much of it remains after accounting for purchase costs?
  • What level of repayment remains comfortable, even if interest rates or household expenses increase?
  • Which essential documents are missing, unclear, or likely to impede the lender's assessment process?
  • Does the property involve unique characteristics such as a new estate, a construction contract, a small lot, potential flood risk, or zoning restrictions that require specific lender scrutiny?

For refinancers, important considerations include the costs involved, the features of alternative loans, and the implications of consolidating debts with a new lender.

Furthermore, the broker should clearly explain their remuneration structure. Typically, most mortgage brokers receive a commission from lenders and do not charge a direct fee to standard home loan customers. Any applicable fees or clawback arrangements should be transparently disclosed upfront.

  2. Assess Repayment Comfort. Evaluate what loan repayment amount feels manageable, considering potential future increases in interest rates or living expenses.
  3. Gather Necessary Documents. Identify and collect all required documentation, ensuring clarity and completeness to avoid assessment delays.
  4. Identify Property-Specific Factors. Note any unique property characteristics (e.g., new estate, construction, flood risk) that may require specific lender attention.
  5. Understand Broker Fees. Clarify how the mortgage broker is compensated and understand any upfront fees or clawback arrangements.
